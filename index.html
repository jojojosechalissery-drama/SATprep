🔥 Harder Concepts

These are the topics where students at 860 usually lose the most points. Try each question before opening the answer. Add every miss to the error log.

How to use this page: Do 2 or 3 concepts per week, then go to the SAT Question Bank, filter by that topic and by Hard difficulty, and do 10 more. Log how many in the "HARD questions" box on the Tracker tab.

📐 Math

Systems with no or infinitely many solutions

Put both equations in the same form. Same slope and same intercept means infinitely many solutions. Same slope but a different intercept means none.

Try it: For what value of k does 3x + ky = 9 together with 6x + 8y = 18 have infinitely many solutions?

Show answer

Divide the second equation by 2 to get 3x + 4y = 9. It matches the first only if k = 4.

Quadratics: vertex, roots and minimum value

Complete the square or use x = −b/(2a) to find the vertex. The vertex gives the minimum or maximum value.

Try it: What is the minimum value of f(x) = x² − 6x + 5?

Show answer

The vertex is at x = 3. f(3) = 9 − 18 + 5 = −4.

Exponential growth and decay

Look for the form y = a·b^(t/period). The base b is the factor per period, and the exponent counts the periods.

Try it: A population of 500 triples every 4 years. How large is it after 8 years?

Show answer

8 years is 2 periods, so 500 × 3² = 4,500.

Circle equations

Complete the square in x and in y to reach (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r².

Try it: Find the center and radius of x² + y² − 6x + 4y = 12.

Show answer

(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 12 + 9 + 4 = 25. The center is (3, −2) and the radius is 5.

Percent change traps

Percent changes multiply and do not add. Always find what the percent is of.

Try it: A price rises 20% and then falls 20%. What is the net change?

Show answer

1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, so the net change is a 4% decrease.

Radical and rational equations (extraneous solutions)

After squaring both sides, always plug your answers back into the original equation.

Try it: Solve √(x + 6) = x.

Show answer

x + 6 = x², so x = 3 or x = −2. Checking shows −2 fails, so x = 3.

Function transformations

f(x − a) moves the graph right by a. f(x) + b moves it up by b. −f(x) flips it over the x-axis.

Try it: How is g(x) = f(x − 3) + 2 related to f(x)?

Show answer

It is shifted 3 units right and 2 units up.

Statistics: what changes when data changes

The mean is pulled by outliers, the median is not. Standard deviation measures spread from the mean.

Try it: For {2, 4, 6, 8, 10}, what happens to the mean and standard deviation if you add another 6?

Show answer

The mean stays 6 and the standard deviation decreases, because the new point sits exactly at the center.

Right-triangle trig

sin A = cos(90° − A). Remember SOH-CAH-TOA, and check whether the problem uses degrees or radians.

Try it: In a right triangle, sin A = 3/5. What is cos(90° − A)?

Show answer

cos(90° − A) = sin A = 3/5.

Desmos shortcuts

Type both equations to find intersections quickly. Use sliders for unknown constants. Click points to see exact values.

Try it: Graph y = x² − 6x + 5 and y = −x + 5. Where do they meet?

Show answer

They meet at (0, 5) and (5, 0). You can check this algebraically: x² − 5x = 0.

📖 Reading & Writing

Run-ons and comma splices

Two complete sentences need a period, a semicolon, or a comma plus a conjunction (FANBOYS). A comma alone is not enough.

Try it: Fix this: 'The experiment failed, the team learned from it.'

Show answer

Use a semicolon: 'The experiment failed; the team learned from it.' A period or a comma plus 'but' also works.

Subject-verb agreement with interruptions

Cross out any phrase between the subject and the verb. The verb agrees with the real subject.

Try it: Choose: 'The box of old letters (is / are) in the attic.'

Show answer

'Is.' The subject is 'box,' not 'letters.'

Modifier placement

An opening phrase must describe the noun that comes right after the comma.

Try it: Fix this: 'Walking down the street, the trees looked beautiful.'

Show answer

The trees were not walking. Better: 'Walking down the street, she thought the trees looked beautiful.'

Pronouns and apostrophes

Its = belongs to it. It's = it is. Whose vs who's follows the same pattern. A pronoun must clearly refer to one noun.

Try it: Choose: 'The company changed (its / it's) policy because (its / it's) customers complained.'

Show answer

'Its policy' (possession) and 'because its customers complained' (possession).

Transitions

Decide the relationship between the two ideas first: contrast, cause, addition, or example. Then pick the matching word.

Try it: 'The data were incomplete. ___, the researchers published the study.' Which fits: Therefore, For example, or However?

Show answer

'However.' The second sentence goes against what you would expect after incomplete data.

Words in context

Cover the word, predict your own word, then choose the closest option. Many words have a second meaning that the SAT likes to test.

Try it: 'The senator's remarks were guarded.' What does 'guarded' most nearly mean?

Show answer

Cautious. It does not mean protected or watched.

Inference traps

Wrong answers are often too extreme (always, never, all), out of scope, or true but not supported by the text.

Try it: A passage says some bees can learn symbols. Which conclusion is best supported: all bees can read, or bees show a capacity for learning?

Show answer

'Bees show a capacity for learning.' The first claim is too extreme.

Command of evidence (graphs and data)

Check every number in each answer choice against the graph. Wrong choices often mix up two categories or years.

Try it: Why is it risky to choose an answer after checking only one number?

Show answer

The choice may contain a second claim that is wrong. Every part of an answer must match the data.

Cross-text connections

Summarize each author's main claim in one phrase. Then decide whether the second author agrees, disagrees, or qualifies the first.

Try it: What is the first step on a paired-passage question?

Show answer

Write a one-phrase summary of each author's position before reading the choices.

Rhetorical synthesis

Reread the goal in the question. The correct answer is the one that serves that specific goal, not just a true statement from the notes.

Try it: If the goal is 'to emphasize the project's cost,' should you choose the statement about its design or its price?

Show answer

Choose the one about its price. It must match the stated goal.

Concision

The shortest answer that keeps the full meaning is usually right. Watch for redundancy.

Try it: Which is better: 'past history' or 'history'?

Show answer

'History.' 'Past' repeats what the word already means.

📋 18-Week Study Plan

An 18-week, step-by-step plan (starting Nov 5) for an 11th grader starting at 860. Target: 1100+ on the March 6, 2027 SAT, with a retake in May or June if needed.

Realistic expectations: Going from 860 to 1100+ (a gain of about 240) is achievable with steady, consistent work, but results vary. Students who review their mistakes carefully improve the most. Aim for steady progress first (1000, then 1100), not perfection.

1. The test at a glance (digital SAT)

Reading & Writing
54 questions, 2 modules, 64 minutes. Short passages, one question each.
Math
44 questions, 2 modules, 70 minutes. Calculator allowed throughout (Desmos is built in).
Scoring
400 to 1600 total. Each section is 200 to 800. The second module adapts to how she does on the first.

The test is taken on a laptop or tablet in the free Bluebook app. Her Huntington test may have been a different format, so her first step is a Bluebook baseline (see Week 1). Please confirm format details at collegeboard.org.

The most important habit: the error log

For every missed or guessed question, write one line: topic, why it was missed (concept gap, careless mistake, ran out of time, misread), and the correct method. Redo each logged question one week later. This is where most of the score improvement comes from.

6. Topic checklists

Math

  • Algebra: linear equations, systems, inequalities, linear word problems
  • Advanced math: quadratics, polynomials, exponentials, function notation, rational and radical equations
  • Problem solving and data: ratios, percents, unit conversion, statistics, probability, scatterplots
  • Geometry and trig: area, volume, triangles, circles, sine/cosine/tangent
  • Tool skills: graph equations in Desmos, find intersections, use sliders

Reading & Writing

  • Craft and structure: vocabulary in context, text structure, cross-text connections
  • Information and ideas: central ideas, evidence, inferences
  • Standard English conventions: punctuation, agreement, tense, pronouns, modifiers
  • Expression of ideas: transitions, rhetorical synthesis
  • Habit: read 15 to 20 minutes a day (news, long-form articles, fiction)

7. Test-taking strategies

  • There is no penalty for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.
  • Math: roughly 1.5 minutes per question. Skip and flag hard ones, then return.
  • Reading & Writing: roughly 1 minute per question. Read the question first, then the passage.
  • For grammar questions, find the rule being tested before looking at the choices.
  • Use elimination: wrong answers usually have a specific flaw (too extreme, off-topic, or unsupported).
  • Do the first module of each section carefully, because it affects how hard the second one is.

✍️ College Essay Prep

Andrea has time. The SAT comes first, and the essay is best written in summer before senior year. Starting small now makes it easier later.

The basics: Most colleges use the Common App, which has one main personal essay of 250 to 650 words. The same essay goes to every college on the Common App, and some colleges also ask for shorter "supplemental" essays. The essay is the one place where admissions readers hear her voice, so it matters.

🗓 Her timeline

Now to Feb 2027 (SAT focus)Keep the SAT first. Spend about 10 minutes a week jotting ideas in the Idea Bank below. Notice moments that matter to you.
Mar to May 2027After the March SAT, start the brainstorming exercises. Read 3 or 4 sample essays. Pick 2 or 3 possible topics.
June to July 2027Write messy first drafts of the 2 best topics. Choose one. Get feedback from a teacher or counselor.
Aug 1, 2027The Common App for her application year opens. Create the account and see that year's prompts (they are usually the same as the year before).
Aug to Sept 2027Revise 2 or 3 times. Read it aloud. Ask 2 trusted readers for feedback.
Oct 2027Final polish and proofread. Most Early Action/Early Decision deadlines are around Nov 1, and Regular Decision deadlines are usually in early January.

🚀 How to get started (step by step)

Step 1: Brainstorm (don't judge yet)

Set a timer for 10 minutes for each of these and write a quick list. Short bullet points are fine.

  • 10 moments that changed how you think or act
  • 5 things you do when no one is making you
  • What do friends and family always say about you?
  • A time you changed your mind about something
  • A small object, place, or routine that matters to you, and why
  • Something you are proud of that is not on your resume
Step 2: Find a story, not a summary

Admissions readers already see her grades, activities and awards. The essay should show who she is. Choose one small moment (a scene) and show what it reveals about her. A small story told well beats a big achievement told in general terms.

Step 3: Make a simple outline
  • Hook: a specific moment or detail (start in the middle of the action)
  • Story: what happened, with details
  • Reflection: what she learned or how she changed
  • Ending: what this means for who she is now and who she wants to be
Step 4: Write a messy first draft

Set a 30-minute timer and write without stopping or editing. It can be 900 words. Cutting is easier than starting. Nobody sees this draft.

Step 5: Revise in layers
  1. Big picture: does it sound like her, and does it have a clear point?
  2. Cut anything generic or repeated.
  3. Replace vague words with specific details.
  4. Read it out loud to hear awkward parts.
  5. Proofread last: spelling, grammar, punctuation, and the word limit.

📝 The 7 Common App prompts

These are the 2026-27 prompts in short form. They have stayed the same for several years, but check commonapp.org for the prompts for her application year. Remember: the story matters more than which prompt she picks.

1. Your background or story. Share something so central to who you are that your application would feel incomplete without it.

2. Challenge or failure. Describe a setback and what you learned from it. Focus on growth, not the hardship itself.

3. Questioning a belief. Write about a time you questioned or challenged an idea, and what happened.

4. Gratitude. Describe something someone did for you that surprised you, and how it affected you.

5. Personal growth. Describe an accomplishment, event or realization that sparked a new understanding of yourself or others.

6. Something you love. Write about a topic or idea that absorbs you so much that you lose track of time, and how you explore it.

7. Topic of your choice. Any essay on any topic, including one you've already written.

Do and avoid

✅ Do
  • Use her own voice and real details
  • Show growth and reflection
  • Focus on one story or theme
  • Be specific: names, places, sounds, moments
  • Ask a teacher or counselor to read it
🚫 Avoid
  • Listing achievements like a resume
  • Writing what she thinks colleges want to hear
  • Topics that are too broad for 650 words
  • Big words just to sound smart
  • Having someone else (or AI) write it. Use help for feedback only, not for writing.

✔ Before she submits

  • ☐ Does it sound like Andrea?
  • ☐ Does it show one clear story or idea?
  • ☐ Is there a specific opening moment?
  • ☐ Does it explain what she learned or how she changed?
  • ☐ Is it under 650 words (aim for 550 to 650)?
  • ☐ Did at least two other people read it?
  • ☐ Was it proofread out loud?

🤝 Volunteer & Leadership Record

Keep one clear record of everything Andrea does, so college applications are easy later. Each entry is saved here and sent to a Google Sheet in your Google Drive.

Why this matters: Students often volunteer for years but forget the dates, hours and responsibilities when applying. The Common App lets her list up to 10 activities, so we record details as they happen.
⚙ One-time setup: connect your Google Sheet

The website can't write to Google Sheets on its own, so you add a small free Google script to your sheet. It takes about 5 minutes.

  1. In Google Drive, create a new Google Sheet named "Andrea Volunteer Record".
  2. In the sheet, click Extensions → Apps Script.
  3. Delete the sample code, paste the code below, and change the secret word on the first line (any word you like).
  4. Click Deploy → New deployment. Choose type Web app. Set "Execute as" to Me and "Who has access" to Anyone. Click Deploy and approve the permissions.
  5. Copy the Web app URL and paste it below along with your secret word. Then click Save.
// Google Apps Script: paste this into Extensions > Apps Script (in your Google Sheet)
var SECRET = 'change-this-to-your-own-secret-word';
var HEADERS = ['Logged on','Organization','Activity','Position','Category','Start date','End date','Hours','Total hours so far','Grades (9-12)','Responsibilities','Impact','Supervisor','Supervisor contact','Short description (150 chars)','Documents saved','Drive folder link','Notes'];

function doPost(e) {
  try {
    var d = JSON.parse(e.postData.contents);
    if (d.key !== SECRET) return ContentService.createTextOutput('denied');
    var ss = SpreadsheetApp.getActiveSpreadsheet();
    var sh = ss.getSheetByName('Volunteer Log') || ss.insertSheet('Volunteer Log');
    if (sh.getLastRow() === 0) {
      sh.appendRow(HEADERS);
      sh.setFrozenRows(1);
      sh.getRange(1, 1, 1, HEADERS.length).setFontWeight('bold');
    }
    sh.appendRow([new Date(), d.org, d.activity, d.position, d.category, d.start, d.end,
      d.hours, d.total, d.grades, d.resp, d.impact, d.sup, d.contact, d.desc, d.docs, d.folder, d.notes]);
    return ContentService.createTextOutput('ok');
  } catch (err) {
    return ContentService.createTextOutput('error: ' + err);
  }
}

Tip: after you add the first entry, check that it appears in the "Volunteer Log" tab of your sheet. If the script is changed later, click Deploy → Manage deployments → edit → new version.
